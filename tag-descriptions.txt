# ---- mechanics: actions and turns ----
Action Drafting = Players pick from a shared, shrinking pool of actions each round.
Action Points = You get a budget of points each turn and spend them on whatever you like.
Action Queue = Actions are lined up in advance and resolve in that order.
Action Retrieval = Used actions are spent until you take a turn to recover them.
Action / Event = Each card is either your action or a game event, never both.
Follow = One player picks an action and everyone else may do a weaker version.
Once-Per-Game Abilities = One-shot powers you can only ever use a single time.
Turn Order: Auction = You bid for where you sit in the turn order.
Turn Order: Claim Action = Taking an action also claims your place in the next round.
Turn Order: Pass Order = The order you passed in sets the next round's order.
Turn Order: Progressive = Turn order advances round to round by a fixed rule.
Turn Order: Role Order = Your chosen role decides when you act.
Turn Order: Stat-Based = Turn order follows some in-game statistic, like money or position.
Turn Order: Time Track = Your turn comes round again based on how long your last action took.
Variable Phase Order = The order of the round's phases changes with player choices.
Simultaneous Action Selection = Everyone chooses secretly, then all reveal at once.
Lose a Turn = Something can cost you your whole turn.
Interrupts = You can act out of turn in response to someone else.

# ---- mechanics: cards ----
Hand Management = Getting value out of an awkward hand is the core puzzle.
Multi-Use Cards = Each card offers several uses and you only get one of them.
Open Drafting = Picking cards from a face-up, shared display.
Closed Drafting = Pick a card from your hand, pass the rest along.
Deck, Bag, and Pool Building = You add and remove cards or tokens to shape what you draw later.
Deck Construction = You build your deck before play, not during it.
Melding and Splaying = Cards are laid out overlapping so only parts stay visible.
Move Through Deck = Progress is measured by how far through a deck you have got.
Card Play Conflict Resolution = Fights are settled by the cards players commit.
Campaign / Battle Card Driven = A deck of cards drives what can happen each turn.
Command Cards = Cards dictate which of your units may act.
Tags = Cards carry keywords that other cards pay off.
Chaining = Each play sets up the next; sequencing is the whole game.
Layering = Pieces or cards stack so earlier ones keep partial effect.

# ---- mechanics: economy ----
Income = You collect a regular, predictable amount each round.
Loans = You can borrow now and pay a penalty later.
Market = A shared market sets prices that everyone's decisions move.
Commodity Speculation = Buy low, sell high on goods whose price shifts.
Stock Holding = You own shares in companies and profit from their performance.
Investment = Putting money in now for a return later.
Ownership = Who owns a thing matters as much as where it sits.
Delayed Purchase = You pay now and receive the goods some turns later.
Increase Value of Unchosen Resources = Whatever nobody takes gets more attractive next time.
Contracts = Open orders you fulfil for a reward.
Victory Points as a Resource = Points double as a currency you can spend.
Automatic Resource Growth = Resources accumulate on their own between turns.
Random Production = How much you produce is down to a die or a draw.
Resource to Move = Moving costs you resources.
Resource Queue = Resources arrive in a fixed order you cannot reorder.
Pick-up and Deliver = Collect goods here, deliver them there, get paid.
Score-and-Reset Game = You score, then much of the board is wiped and begun again.

# ---- mechanics: auctions ----
Auction / Bidding = Players bid against each other for what is on offer.
Constrained Bidding = Your bid must follow a restrictive rule, not just be higher.
Closed Economy Auction = The money in the auction only circulates among players.
Selection Order Bid = You bid for the right to choose first.
Predictive Bid = You bid on what you think will happen.
Auction: Turn Order Until Pass = Bidding goes round until everyone but one has passed.
Auction: Fixed Placement = You place bids on specific spots rather than raising freely.
Auction: Once Around = Each player bids exactly once, in order.
Auction: Dutch = The price falls until somebody takes it.
Auction: Sealed Bid = Everyone bids in secret and all reveal together.
Auction: Multiple Lot = Several lots are auctioned at the same time.
Force Commitment = You must commit your strength before knowing the outcome.

# ---- mechanics: placement and space ----
Worker Placement = You place limited workers on action spaces; taken spaces block others.
Worker Placement, Different Worker Types = Your workers are not interchangeable.
Worker Placement with Dice Workers = Dice act as workers and their faces limit what they can do.
Tile Placement = You add tiles to a growing layout.
Modular Board = The board is assembled from pieces, so it differs every game.
Map Addition = The map grows as the game goes on.
Map Reduction = The map shrinks as the game goes on.
Map Deformation = The map itself moves or changes shape.
Multiple Maps = Play spans more than one board.
Hexagon Grid = Play happens on a hex grid.
Square Grid = Play happens on a square grid.
Grid Coverage = Filling space on a grid efficiently is the puzzle.
Enclosure = You score by surrounding or fencing off areas.
Area Majority / Influence = Whoever has the most presence in a region takes the reward.
Area Movement = Pieces move between defined regions rather than squares.
Area-Impulse = A round is broken into impulses of activity rather than clean turns.
Zone of Control = Enemy pieces restrict movement around them.
Line of Sight = What a piece can reach depends on what it can see.
Point to Point Movement = Movement follows a network of connected points.
Grid Movement = Pieces move square by square.
Movement Points = Each move costs from a movement allowance.
Relative Movement = Position is tracked relative to other pieces, not on a map.
Three Dimensional Movement = Movement includes height as well as position.
Pattern Movement = Pieces move in prescribed shapes.
Programmed Movement = You plan several moves in advance and then watch them play out.
Slide / Push = Pieces slide or shove others along.
Mancala = You pick up a group of pieces and sow them one at a time.
Network and Route Building = Building connected routes across the board.
Connections = Scoring comes from linking things together.
Chaining = Each play sets up the next; sequencing is the whole game.
Static Capture = Pieces are captured without moving onto them.
Secret Unit Deployment = Your forces are hidden from opponents.
Hidden Movement = One side moves unseen and the others hunt them.
Advantage Token = A token passes around granting whoever holds it an edge.
Tug of War = A shared marker slides back and forth between the sides.
King of the Hill = Holding a central position is what wins.
Race = First to the end of a track wins.
Catch the Leader = The rules actively help whoever is behind.
Sudden Death Ending = The game can end abruptly when a condition is met.
Finale Ending = A distinct final phase decides things.
Elapsed Real Time Ending = The game ends when the clock runs out.

# ---- mechanics: dice and luck ----
Dice Rolling = Dice are central to resolving actions.
Die Icon Resolution = Dice show symbols rather than numbers.
Re-rolling and Locking = You may reroll some dice and keep others.
Push Your Luck = Keep going for more, risk losing what you have.
Critical Hits and Failures = Extreme rolls have outsized effects.
Random Production = How much you produce is down to a die or a draw.
Dice Drafting = Dice are rolled into a pool and players pick from it.

# ---- mechanics: social ----
Negotiation = Players openly bargain and make deals.
Trading = Players swap resources with each other.
Bribery = You can pay other players to act in your interest.
Alliances = Players form and break formal partnerships.
Voting = The group votes and the result binds everyone.
Betting and Bluffing = Wagering on outcomes while misleading others.
Bluffing = Succeeding by misrepresenting what you hold or intend.
Hidden Roles = Some players have secret identities with different goals.
Traitor Game = Someone at the table is secretly working against the rest.
Roles with Asymmetric Information = Players know different things by design.
Team-Based Game = Players win or lose in teams.
Communication Limits = What you may say to your partners is restricted.
Targeted Clues = You give clues aimed at a specific player.
Storytelling = Players invent and tell a narrative.
Singing = Yes, really: you have to sing.
Deduction = Working out hidden information from what you can see.
Memory = Remembering what you have seen is the challenge.
Player Elimination = Players can be knocked out before the end.
Take That = Direct, often nasty attacks on a specific opponent.
Kill Steal = You get the reward for finishing something off, not for the work.
Stealing = Taking things directly from another player.
Cooperative Game = Everyone plays together against the game.
Semi-Cooperative Game = Players cooperate but only one can win outright.
Real-Time = Play happens continuously, without turns.
Speed Matching = Spotting and claiming matches faster than anyone else.
Bias = The system nudges outcomes in a consistent direction.

# ---- mechanics: scoring and structure ----
End Game Bonuses = Extra points are handed out for various things once play ends.
Hidden Victory Points = Some of everyone's score is concealed until the end.
Highest-Lowest Scoring = Your worst category drags your score; balance is everything.
Set Collection = Points come from gathering matching or complementary sets.
Pattern Building = Arranging pieces into specific shapes scores.
Pattern Recognition = Spotting a pattern quickly is the skill.
Matching = Pairing like with like.
Ordering = Putting things in the right sequence.
Neighbor Scope = Only what is adjacent to you matters.
Tech Trees / Tech Tracks = You unlock improvements along branching tracks.
Track Movement = Progress is marked by advancing along tracks.
Rondel = A wheel of actions; moving further round it costs more.
Variable Player Powers = Each player has different abilities from the start.
Variable Set-up = The starting position changes from game to game.
Events = Scheduled or random events change the situation mid-game.
Solo / Solitaire Game = The game supports a single-player mode.
Scenario / Mission / Campaign Game = Play is structured as scenarios, often linked.
Legacy Game = The game permanently changes between sessions.
Narrative Choice / Paragraph = You read passages and choose how the story continues.
Role Playing = You take on a character rather than an abstract position.
Simulation = The rules aim to model something real.
Paper-and-Pencil = You record your progress by writing it down.
Bingo = Marking off results as they are drawn.
Square Grid = Play happens on a square grid.
Closed Economy Auction = The money in the auction only circulates among players.

BGG snapshot __STAMP__ — ranks, weights, play counts and player-count polls from boardgamegeek.com; thumbnails embedded in the file. Interaction and point-salad columns are derived locally and are opinions, not BGG data.

Columns
Rank
BGG's overall rank. Unranked games sort last.
BGG · Mine
BGG's average rating, and your own rating from your collection. Both are out of 10.
Plays
Plays you have logged on BGG.
Search
Looks at everything on the row and in its expanded panel: the name, the year, the designers and publisher, every property, how the game is won and how much of a salad it is, your own comment on the collection entry — “(shrink)” finds what is still sealed, “(open)” what is not — plus BGG's other titles for the game, which are not shown. Every word has to land somewhere, but not together and not in order — “board dice” finds the Board&Dice games. Accents are optional: “orleans” finds Orléans. What matched is highlighted when you open the row.
“id:1234” is a word too, and matches only the game with that BGG id — which is what a “you might also like” pill types for you, so it lands on one game rather than on everything that mentions its name.
Game
The columns button above the table, at the right, decides what rides along with the name: nothing, the year, the designer, or both. Whatever the name carries loses its own column. Designer also folds back under the name when the window is too narrow to hold its column.
Last played
The most recent date in your BGG play log.
Win scores
The range a winning score typically falls in, blank where a score is not what decides the game. Off by default, and only filled in when the optional enrichment step has been run.
Edited
When the collection entry itself last changed on BGG — a rating, a play count, anything. Not when you got the game.
Acquired · Price paid
Both live in the private part of a collection entry, which the API never returns. They come from a CSV export instead — download it from your BGG collection page and save it as data/collection.csv. That file is gitignored and read only while a report is being written, so these two columns exist only in reports you build yourself: nothing private reaches the saved data or a hosted copy.
Weight
BGG's complexity vote on a 1–5 scale: a bar from green for light to red for heavy, and the band it falls in — Light, Medium light, Medium, Heavy, Extreme — cut at 1.5, 2.5 and 3.5, the midpoints between the points BGG's own Game Weight poll names, and at 4.2, where the handful of games people call unplayably heavy start. Whether you see the band above the number or the number alone is the Cells setting in the columns menu — detailed or compact — and it is the same for every column that has both to show.
Time
The box's own range, banded by its upper bound — at a game night the question is how long this could run, not how quickly it could be over. Very short tops out under half an hour, Short by 45 min, Medium by 90, Long by two hours, Very long by three, and Extreme is anything past that.
Interaction
How much the players' games touch each other — Low, Medium or High — and under it, what kind: direct attacks, area control, a shared market, blocking, an auction, and so on. Neither is a BGG statistic; see below.
At N
What the player-count poll says about the count you picked. Best at N = N drew more “Best” votes than any other count. Good at N = best, or ≥50% of voters at N called N the best count. Under the pill sits the whole vote in one bar, and the figure beside it reads best — the share calling it the best count — then recommended, the share that would still sit down for it. The columns menu offers those two as columns of their own, Best at N and Appr at N, where approval is (Best + Recommended) ÷ all votes at that count. All three are hidden while the count is “Any”, because there is nothing for them to read.
Votes
How many people have rated the game on BGG — a rough proxy for how well known it is.
Filters worth explaining
Rule
How strict the player count is: plays at it, good at it, or best at it. N/A until a count is chosen.
Interaction, Point salad, Weight, Time
Each opens a two-handled range over the underlying number — weight is 1–5, time is minutes, point salad is its 0–100 score — with the bands marked along it as dots and named beside them. So you can ask for “anything up to 90 minutes” or “3.4 and heavier”, not just whole bands. Interaction is Low, Medium or High with nothing in between, so it snaps to those three. A range covering everything is the filter having no opinion. ticking Light and Heavy shows games that are either, not games that are somehow both. Ticking nothing is the filter having no opinion. The title at the top of the page clears every filter, the sort and any open game at once, and leaves your column choices alone.
Expansions
Hidden by default but still counted: an expansion's own poll can be what makes the base game good at a count.
Win condition
What actually decides the winner — most points, a race, elimination, and so on.
Point salad
How many different things earn you points: no — a sharp focus, just a touch, quite a lot, or a total point salad. The band is cut from an underlying 0-100 score, and the columns menu offers the band, the score, or one column carrying both. The filter always uses the bands.
Approved at N
Whether half the voters at that count think the game works there at all — a yes/no question, so the pill answers yes or no rather than repeating the Best/Good/Plays verdict beside it.
Properties
One field over everything a game is tagged with — BGG's categories, mechanics, families and designers, how the game is won and what kind of interaction it has, plus a few named groupings of mechanics — searchable by name or by description. “8 of 41” counts matches within the filters already applied. Match ticked properties by all, any, or none.
Where the data comes from

Everything above is BGG's own data, cached locally — every filter runs in this page and nothing calls out to BGG.

Three fields are not BGG's: Interaction, Win condition and Point salad, along with the “What people say” notes in an expanded row, come from a local database built with Claude from what is known about each game (a few looked up on the web, marked where so). No such data exists in BGG's API. Interaction is the phrase players keep using about a game — “multiplayer solitaire”, “cutthroat”, “a race you watch” — turned into a level; Win condition and Point salad are computed from reported facts about how a game is scored, not asked for directly. Treat them as a rough sort, not fact; where the database has nothing, Interaction falls back to a rule over the game's mechanics.

The filters, the sort and the game you have expanded are kept in the page's address as you set them, so the link in your address bar is the view you are looking at — copy it to send someone exactly this table, or one game in it. The back button closes an expanded game rather than leaving the page. Reset filters clears it again.

Click any row to expand it. Pills inside filter the table to that designer, mechanic or game — except games you do not own, which link out to BGG.

The columns button at the right of the count line turns columns on and off; drag a column's header to move it, and "Reset to default" puts everything back. Your choices are remembered in this browser.

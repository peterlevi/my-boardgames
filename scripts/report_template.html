Columns
Rank
BGG's overall rank. Unranked games sort last.
BGG · Mine
BGG's average rating, and your own rating from your collection. Both are out of 10.
Plays
Plays you have logged on BGG.
Search
Looks at everything on the row and in its expanded panel: the name, the year, the designers and publisher, every property, how the game is won and how much of a salad it is. What matched is highlighted when you open the row.
Game
The columns button above the table, at the right, decides what rides along with the name: nothing, the year, the designer, or both. Whatever the name carries loses its own column. Designer also folds back under the name when the window is too narrow to hold its column.
Last played
The most recent date in your BGG play log.
Winning score
The range a winning score typically falls in, blank where a score is not what decides the game. Off by default, and only filled in when the optional enrichment step has been run.
Edited
When the collection entry itself last changed on BGG — a rating, a play count, anything. Not when you got the game.
Acquired · Price paid
Both live in the private part of a collection entry, which the API never returns. They come from a CSV export instead — download it from your BGG collection page and save it as data/collection.csv. That file is gitignored and read only while a report is being written, so these two columns exist only in reports you build yourself: nothing private reaches the saved data or a hosted copy.
Weight
BGG's complexity vote on a 1–5 scale, shown as a bar: green is light, red is heavy.
Interaction
How much the players' games touch each other — Low, Medium or High. Not a BGG statistic; see below.
At N
What the player-count poll says about the count you picked. Best at N = N drew more “Best” votes than any other count. Good at N = best, or ≥50% of voters at N called N the best count.
Best · Appr
At the chosen count: the share of voters calling it the best count, and approval = (Best + Recommended) ÷ all votes at that count. Blank until you pick a count.
Ratings
How many people have rated the game on BGG — a rough proxy for how well known it is.
Filters worth explaining
Rule
How strict the player count is: plays at it, good at it, or best at it. N/A until a count is chosen.
Expansions
Hidden by default but still counted: an expansion's own poll can be what makes the base game good at a count.
Win condition
What actually decides the winner — most points, a race, elimination, and so on.
Point salad
How many different things earn you points: no — a sharp focus, just a touch, quite a lot, or a total point salad.
Properties
One field over everything a game is tagged with — BGG's categories, mechanics, families and designers, plus a few named groupings of mechanics — searchable by name or by description. “8 of 41” counts matches within the filters already applied. Match ticked properties by all, any, or none.
Where the data comes from

Everything above is BGG's own data, cached locally — every filter runs in this page and nothing calls out to BGG.

Three fields are not BGG's: Interaction, Win condition and Point salad, along with the “What people say” notes in an expanded row, come from a local database built with Claude from what is known about each game (a few looked up on the web, marked where so). No such data exists in BGG's API. Treat them as a rough sort, not fact; where the database has nothing, Interact falls back to a rule over the game's mechanics.

Click any row to expand it. Pills inside filter the table to that designer, mechanic or game — except games you do not own, which link out to BGG.

The columns button at the right of the count line turns columns on and off and says what the Game column carries; drag a column's header to move it, and "Reset to default" puts everything back. Your choices are remembered in this browser.
